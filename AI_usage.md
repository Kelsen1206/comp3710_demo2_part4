## 2. VAE architecture and training script

The initial implementation of the encoder/decoder architecture, the reparameterisation
trick, the BCE + KL divergence loss, the training loop, and the three visualisations
(loss curve, reconstruction comparison, latent manifold sweep) was generated by Claude
as part of that conversation.

Before delivering the code, Claude ran a forward and backward pass of the architecture
against the actual OASIS slices I had uploaded, to confirm correct tensor shapes and
that gradients computed successfully. I verified the results independently by running
the full training on Rangpur.

**What I am responsible for and can explain:**

- Why the KL divergence term is what makes the latent space continuous, and therefore
  what makes interpolating between latent points produce smoothly varying brain slices
  rather than disconnected outputs.
- Why the reparameterisation trick is necessary — sampling is not differentiable, so
  the randomness is moved into a separate `eps` term to allow gradients to flow back
  through the encoder.
- Why a separate 2-dimensional latent model is trained purely for the manifold
  visualisation (a 16-dimensional latent space cannot be swept and rendered directly
  as a 2D image grid).
- Why VAE reconstructions are characteristically blurry — the tradeoff between the
  reconstruction and KL terms in the loss.

## 4. Interpreting the results

I ran the training to completion and shared the real output (loss values across 40
epochs and the three generated figures) back with Claude to verifying result's correctness.

Points raised in that discussion that I have carried into my own understanding:

- Train loss (1036) and validation loss (1053) tracking closely together indicates the
  model is generalising rather than memorising the training slices — if it were
  overfitting, validation loss would begin rising while training loss continued falling.
- The loss plateau from around epoch 30 indicates training ran long enough, rather than
  stalling prematurely.
- **The degraded, noisy samples in the upper-right of the manifold grid** are not a bug.
  That region is far from the origin of the latent space, where the KL prior concentrates
  the training encodings — so the decoder is extrapolating into a region it saw little
  data from, rather than interpolating between known points.
