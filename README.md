# COMP3710 Lab Demo 2 — Part 4, Task 1: Variational Autoencoder on OASIS Brain MRI

A convolutional Variational Autoencoder (VAE) trained on the Preprocessed OASIS brain MRI
dataset, with a visualisation of the learned latent manifold.

## Problem

Learn a compact, continuous latent representation of 2D axial brain MRI slices, such that
new brain images can be generated by sampling from the latent space, and such that moving
smoothly through that space produces smoothly varying anatomy.

## Dataset

Preprocessed OASIS MRI slices (`/home/groups/comp3710/` on the UQ Rangpur cluster).

| Split | Slices |
|---|---|
| Train | 9,664 |
| Validation | 1,120 |

Images are loaded as 8-bit grayscale PNGs, resized to 64×64, and scaled to `[0, 1]`.

## Architecture

**Encoder** — two strided convolutions (64×64 → 32×32 → 16×16, channels 1 → 32 → 64),
flattened into two parallel fully-connected heads producing the latent mean (`mu`) and
log-variance (`logvar`).

**Reparameterisation** — samples `z = mu + eps * std` where `eps ~ N(0, 1)`. This keeps the
sampling step differentiable so gradients can flow back through the encoder during training.

**Decoder** — mirrors the encoder: a fully-connected layer back to 64×16×16, then two
transposed convolutions (16×16 → 32×32 → 64×64), with a sigmoid output so pixel values
land in `[0, 1]` to match the input range.

**Loss** — binary cross-entropy reconstruction term plus KL divergence against a standard
normal prior. The KL term is what makes the latent space continuous and well-organised
rather than a set of scattered, disconnected codes, which is what makes meaningful
interpolation between points possible.

## Training

- Optimiser: Adam, learning rate 1e-3
- Batch size: 64
- Epochs: 40
- Hardware: single NVIDIA A100 (Rangpur `comp3710` partition), submitted via Slurm

Two models are trained:
1. A 16-dimensional latent model, used for the reconstruction comparison.
2. A separate 2-dimensional latent model, so the latent space can be swept and rendered
   directly as a 2D grid of decoded images.

## Results

**Convergence** (`part4_loss_curve.png`): train loss 1213 → 1036, validation loss 1107 → 1053
over 40 epochs, plateauing around epoch 30. Train and validation losses track closely
throughout, indicating the model generalises rather than memorising the training slices.

**Reconstructions** (`part4_reconstructions.png`): ventricular structure, skull outline and
overall brain shape are reproduced accurately. Fine cortical texture is smoothed — the
expected blurriness of a VAE, arising from the tradeoff between the reconstruction and
KL terms in the loss.

**Latent manifold** (`part4_manifold.png`): decoding a 12×12 grid over the 2D latent space
produces brain slices that vary smoothly and continuously in both directions, with ventricle
shape and overall slice appearance morphing gradually rather than jumping between unrelated
images. Samples far from the latent origin (upper-right of the grid) degrade into noise,
since the KL prior concentrates the training encodings near the origin and the decoder is
extrapolating beyond the region it saw during training.

## Running

On Rangpur:

```bash
conda activate torch
sbatch vae_job.sh
squeue --me
```

Locally (CPU, for prototyping): edit `train_dir` / `val_dir` near the top of
`part4_vae_rangpur.py` to point at a local copy of the dataset, and optionally pass
`limit=` to `OASISSliceDataset` to use a small subset.
